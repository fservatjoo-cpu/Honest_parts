Yangwang U9 Xtreme: The World's Fastest Production Car?

An electric hypercar from BYD's luxury brand, Yangwang, reached 496.22 km/h (308.4 mph) in 2025. That is faster than any other production car on record, according to the company.

Why It's So Fast

The Yangwang U9 Xtreme is not a Bugatti, a Koenigsegg, or a Ferrari. It is an all-electric car, and it beat the best gasoline hypercars on top speed. The earlier record holder, the Bugatti Chiron Super Sport 300+, reached about 490 km/h. The U9 Xtreme went past it at 496 km/h, and reports say it still had speed left on the short test track.

The car is built on BYD's e4 platform, with one electric motor at each wheel. Together the four motors make around 3,000 horsepower in the Xtreme version. The car weighs about 2.5 tons (2,475 kg), which is very heavy for a sports car. To handle that weight, it uses an active suspension called DiSus-X. The system controls each wheel separately to keep the body stable. BYD has even shown the car driving on three wheels and jumping over bumps.

About BYD and Yangwang

BYD was founded in 1995 in Shenzhen, China, as a maker of rechargeable batteries. It entered the car business in 2003 and has grown very fast. BYD now employs more than one million people, and it says over 110,000 of them are engineers, the largest research team of any car company. It sold about 4.6 million vehicles in 2025.

Yangwang is BYD's high-end luxury and performance brand. It makes the U8 SUV and the U9 sports car, and its cars cost far more than regular BYD models.

Why It Matters

BYD is known for building electric cars that cost less than many Western brands, and it is putting pressure on traditional car makers around the world. Its cars are sold in many countries in Asia, Europe, and South America. They are not sold in the US because of high tariffs and other rules. Canada has opened its market, and BYD plans to launch there in late 2026.

In short, a Chinese electric hypercar now holds a top speed record, and the company behind it is changing the global car industry.

The Fine Print

The 496 km/h run was driven in one direction only. An official top-speed record usually needs the average of two runs in opposite directions, so some car fans say the SSC Tuatara (455.3 km/h) still holds the "official" record. The previous well-known record holder, the Bugatti Chiron Super Sport 300+, reached about 490 km/h.

These facts come from news reports and BYD's own announcements in 2025. Car records change fast, so check a recent source before you quote them.
