Get Your Car Ready for Winter: 9 Things to Do Before the Cold Hits

F
FabioHonest Ride Parts

Hi, it's Fabio from Honest Ride Parts.

Before the cold weather really hits, spend a little time getting your car ready. You'll be less likely to get stuck, and less likely to end up with a rusty or broken car by spring. Here are nine things to do now.

  1. 1 of 9

    Test your battery

    Cold weather is hard on batteries, and an old one can quit on you once it gets freezing. Look at the date on the battery. If it's three to five years old, think about replacing it. Better yet, take it to an auto parts store and have them test it for free so you know it's still strong.

    Shop car batteriesShop jumper cables
  2. 2 of 9

    Check all your fluids

    Make sure the oil level is good. In very cold places you might need a thinner oil, so check your owner's manual. Check the coolant while the engine is cold and make sure it's mixed so it won't freeze. Also switch to a windshield washer fluid that won't freeze.

    Safety tip: Never open the radiator or coolant cap while the engine is hot.

    Shop antifreezeShop winter washer fluid
  3. 3 of 9

    Replace worn wiper blades

    Your wiper blades matter more in winter because of all the salt and grime. Old blades just smear everything around instead of cleaning it, so put new ones on if yours are worn out.

    Shop winter wiper blades
  4. 4 of 9

    Get your tires ready

    Winter tires give you much better grip on snow and ice than regular all-season tires. Even if you stick with all-seasons, check the air pressure and make sure there's still decent tread left. Worn tires don't hold the road well in slippery conditions.

    Good to know: Tire pressure drops about 1 PSI for every 10°F the temperature falls, so check it again after the first cold morning.

    Shop tire pressure gaugesShop tread depth gauges
  5. 5 of 9

    Do bigger repairs now

    Do any bigger repairs or maintenance before winter arrives. It's a lot harder and more miserable to work on your car once it's freezing outside.

  6. 6 of 9

    Test your heater and defrosters

    Test your heater before you need it. Run the fan on different speeds, turn the temperature from cold to hot, and make sure the defrosters and seat heaters (if you have them) are working.

  7. 7 of 9

    Keep your gas tank at least half full

    A fuller tank leaves less room for moisture, which helps keep water from forming inside and freezing the fuel lines. It also means you can keep the engine running longer for heat if you ever get stuck.

    Safety tip: If you're stuck in snow with the engine running, keep the tailpipe clear of snow. A blocked tailpipe can push dangerous carbon monoxide into the car.

  8. 8 of 9

    Protect your car from road salt

    Road salt is tough on cars and makes rust happen faster. An undercoating on the bottom of the vehicle creates a barrier that helps protect it. A ceramic spray or similar coating on the paint also makes salt and dirt rinse off more easily, so your car stays nicer longer.
